Before starting FvF Bellows, please review what training an AI on your own hardware involves.

Important Warnings & Expectations

  • You are training an AI on your computer - Everything runs locally, nothing is sent to the cloud.
  • System lag during training - Training an AI is an intensive process, it will push your GPU/CPU and RAM near capacity. Your computer will likely feel very sluggish and hard to use for other tasks until finished. While training is running, Bellows will prevent your computer from automatically going to sleep and pauses normal Fire vs Fire page scanning. You can close this tab and reopen live progress anytime from Fire vs Fire Options. Training will not start unless your device is plugged in.
  • Estimated time on your hardware: Detecting your hardware… (typically between several minutes and a few hours). For heavy models or CPU-only laptops, consider letting it run for a few hours and doing something else, or use a different device.
  • Temporary disk space required: Needs ~1.2 GB free temporarily for Image training or ~0.9 GB for Text to store the base web dataset, ethical guard, and training caches.
  • Results are not guaranteed: Depending on your topic and examples, the classifier might make mistakes, have false alarms, or even fail completely to learn the pattern.
  • Runtime impact per classifier: Every active classifier you add increases the work each time you open or scroll a page, meaning classification will be slower and use more power.

    How Your AI Is Being Made

    1. Feature Extraction

    Instead of starting from zero, Bellows starts with a backbone that already understands edges, textures, shapes, or grammar, and adapts its final representations to your topic.

    2. Backpropagation & AFPL

    Each batch measures how wrong the prediction was. False alarms on normal web pages receive extra penalty so your classifier stays conservative while browsing.

    3. Held-Out Validation

    18% of your examples are hidden during gradient updates and tested after every epoch—preventing memorization and picking the checkpoint with the best real-world accuracy.
